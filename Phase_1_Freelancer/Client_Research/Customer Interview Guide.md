# Customer Interview Guide

**Version:** 1.0\
**Status:** Founding Research Playbook

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Purpose

This guide defines how we interview customers to discover real problems,
validate assumptions, and uncover opportunities worth building.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Interview Philosophy

We are not selling.

We are learning.

Our objective is to understand the customer's world---not convince them
that our idea is good.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Interview Goals

Every interview should help us understand:

-   Daily workflow
-   Biggest frustrations
-   Current solutions
-   Business impact
-   Buying behavior
-   Desired outcomes

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Before the Interview

## Preparation Checklist

-   Define the research objective.
-   Review the customer's business.
-   Prepare open-ended questions.
-   Avoid discussing solutions too early.
-   Allocate 30--60 minutes.
-   Ask permission to take notes or record.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Interview Structure

## Stage 1 --- Build Rapport (5--10 min)

Example questions:

-   Tell me about your business.
-   What does a typical day look like?
-   What are your main responsibilities?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Stage 2 --- Understand the Workflow

Questions:

-   Walk me through your current process.
-   Which tasks take the most time?
-   Which tasks are repetitive?
-   Where do mistakes happen?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Stage 3 --- Discover Problems

Questions:

-   What's your biggest challenge today?
-   What frustrates you the most?
-   How often does this happen?
-   What happens if it's not solved?

Always ask: **"Why?"** several times to reach the root cause.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Stage 4 --- Current Solutions

Questions:

-   How do you solve this today?
-   What tools do you use?
-   What works well?
-   What doesn't?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Stage 5 --- Business Impact

Questions:

-   How much time does this cost?
-   Does it affect revenue?
-   Does it affect customers?
-   How do you measure success?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Stage 6 --- Buying Behavior

Questions:

-   Have you paid to solve this before?
-   What would make you switch?
-   Who approves purchases?
-   What concerns you before buying?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Stage 7 --- Closing

Ask:

-   Is there anything we didn't discuss?
-   What's the one improvement you'd make today?
-   Can we contact you again for follow-up?

Thank them sincerely.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Interview Rules

-   Listen more than you speak.
-   Ask open-ended questions.
-   Never defend your idea.
-   Never interrupt.
-   Stay curious.
-   Ask for examples.
-   Validate understanding by summarizing.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Biases to Avoid

-   Leading questions
-   Confirmation bias
-   Talking too much
-   Assuming instead of asking
-   Ignoring contradictory evidence

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Notes Template

Capture:

-   Customer Profile
-   Problems Mentioned
-   Frequency
-   Business Impact
-   Current Solution
-   Quotes
-   Opportunities
-   Follow-up Actions

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Success Criteria

A successful interview produces new insights, validates or disproves
assumptions, and identifies opportunities backed by evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Final Principle

Customers rarely ask for the right product.

They describe their problems.

Our responsibility is to discover the solution.
